How the matching works

A lost report and a found report are compared as a pair. A classifier learns, from labelled pairs, which combinations of clues usually mean "same item".

The clues

  • Words. Descriptions are lower-cased and stripped of special characters, then turned into TF-IDF vectors. Cosine similarity, word overlap and colour agreement or conflict are measured.
  • Category. Whether both reporters chose the same category. "Not sure" counts as no information.
  • Place. The network has {{ n_stations }} stations ({{ n_interchange }} interchanges). Distance is the number of stops on the shortest route, so a wallet handed in two stops away still scores well.
  • Time. An item cannot be found before it was lost, and finds soon after the loss are likelier.

The ranking score

Candidates are ranked by S = {{ cfg.weights.model }}·P + {{ cfg.weights.text }}·C + {{ cfg.weights.location }}·L + {{ cfg.weights.time }}·T, where P is the model's match probability, C the text similarity, L closeness in stops (1 at the same station, 0 at 8 or more stops) and T timing. The site shows S × 10 as a score out of 10, so 0.78 appears as 7.8. The weights were picked from six candidates on {{ 200 }} validation searches. The blended options landed within about three points of each other, so treat these weights as reasonable defaults, not optimal ones.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The training data is synthetic but deliberately hard: look-alike non-matches (often the same colour), pairs of similar items that text cannot separate, vague reports, colour mix-ups such as black versus navy, and wrong categories. Dates give no clue, and no item combination appears in both training and test. Matching items are still closer in place, as they would be in real life, and real reports will behave differently.
  • The station list is a subset typed from public line maps. Check it against the official map before real use.
  • Wording the model has not seen, spelling mistakes and unusual synonyms are weak spots. A stronger text model could help once real data exists.
  • A high score is a lead, not proof. The desk verifies ownership, so descriptions here hide numbers and email addresses, and no contact details are collected.
